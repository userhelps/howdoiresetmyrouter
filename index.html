How Do I Reset My Router? - Router Reset & Troubleshooting Guide

Having trouble with your internet connection? Resetting your router can often help resolve slow internet speeds, connection drops, Wi-Fi problems, and other common network issues. This guide explains how to restart or reset your router safely and what to check if the problem continues.

Common Router Problems

  • Wi-Fi is connected but there is no internet access.
  • Internet connection keeps dropping.
  • Router is running slowly or becoming unresponsive.
  • Devices cannot connect to the Wi-Fi network.
  • Wi-Fi speeds are slower than expected.
  • Router settings need to be restored or refreshed.

How Do I Restart My Router?

Quick Restart Steps:

  1. Turn off your router using the power button, if available.
  2. Unplug the router's power cable from the electrical outlet.
  3. Wait approximately 30 seconds.
  4. Plug the power cable back into the outlet.
  5. Turn the router back on if it has a power button.
  6. Wait several minutes for the router to fully reconnect to the internet.
  7. Reconnect your phone, computer, or other devices to the Wi-Fi network.

How Do I Factory Reset My Router?

A factory reset is different from a normal restart. It erases customized settings such as your Wi-Fi name, Wi-Fi password, and other router configurations and returns the router to its original factory settings.

Important: Only perform a factory reset if you are sure you know how to set up your router again. You may need your internet provider's connection information and the router's default login details afterward.

Factory Reset Steps:

  1. Keep the router powered on.
  2. Locate the small reset button, usually found on the back or bottom of the router.
  3. Use a paperclip or similar object to press and hold the reset button.
  4. Hold the button for the amount of time specified by your router manufacturer.
  5. Release the button and wait for the router to restart.
  6. Reconnect to the router using its default Wi-Fi information.
  7. Reconfigure your Wi-Fi name, password, and other network settings as needed.

What Should I Do If My Router Still Does Not Work?

  • Check that all power and Ethernet cables are firmly connected.
  • Make sure your internet service is active.
  • Restart both your modem and router if you use separate devices.
  • Try connecting another device to determine whether the problem affects one device or the entire network.
  • Check the router's status lights for unusual indicators.
  • Move closer to the router to test whether the issue is related to Wi-Fi signal strength.
  • Contact your internet service provider if the connection remains unavailable.

Restart vs. Factory Reset

A normal restart simply powers the router off and back on and usually does not erase your settings. A factory reset restores the router to its default configuration and may require you to set up your network again.

Final Advice

For most common internet connection problems, start with a simple router restart before performing a factory reset. A restart is quick and normally preserves your existing network settings. If the issue continues, check your cables, modem, Wi-Fi settings, and internet service before attempting a factory reset.
